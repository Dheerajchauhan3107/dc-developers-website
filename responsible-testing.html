Responsible Testing Policy

Last Updated: 19 November 2026

1. Our Commitment

DC DEVELOPERS supports responsible cybersecurity research, ethical security testing, and digital safety awareness.

We believe security testing should be conducted transparently, lawfully, and with respect for system owners, users, and their data.

2. Authorization Is Mandatory

Security testing must only be performed on systems, websites, applications, APIs, or networks for which explicit authorization has been obtained from the owner or an authorized representative.

Important: Public accessibility does not mean permission to test a system. Never assume that a publicly accessible website or application is authorized for security testing.

3. Written Permission and Scope

Before beginning a professional security assessment, the testing parties should establish written authorization and an agreed scope of work.

The agreement should clearly identify:

  • The systems, domains, applications, and APIs included in the assessment.
  • The authorized testing period.
  • The permitted testing activities.
  • Activities that are explicitly prohibited.
  • Emergency contacts and communication procedures.
  • Data handling and reporting requirements.

Testing must remain within the approved scope. Any change in scope requires additional authorization.

4. Safe Testing Practices

Authorized testing should be conducted carefully to minimize risk to systems, services, and users.

  • Avoid unnecessary disruption of services.
  • Do not access, modify, or expose unrelated user data.
  • Do not perform activities outside the approved scope.
  • Use controlled test environments whenever possible.
  • Stop testing if unexpected impact or instability occurs.
  • Keep assessment information confidential.

5. Prohibited Activities

DC DEVELOPERS does not authorize or encourage illegal or harmful cyber activities.

The following activities are prohibited:

  • Unauthorized access to systems or accounts.
  • Data theft, exfiltration, or unauthorized disclosure.
  • Deploying malware or ransomware.
  • Disrupting services or intentionally damaging systems.
  • Accessing or modifying another person's information without permission.
  • Using security findings for extortion, threats, or harassment.
  • Testing third-party infrastructure outside the approved scope.

6. Vulnerability Reporting

If you identify a potential security vulnerability in a system, report it responsibly to the relevant system owner or their designated security contact.

A responsible report should include:

  • A brief description of the suspected vulnerability.
  • The affected website, application, or component.
  • The potential security impact.
  • Relevant non-sensitive evidence.
  • Suggested remediation, if known.

Do not include passwords, private user data, or other sensitive information in a report unless a secure and authorized process specifically requires it.

7. Responsible Disclosure

Security findings should be communicated privately to the affected organization before any public disclosure.

Researchers should allow the organization a reasonable opportunity to investigate and address the issue.

Public disclosure should avoid exposing sensitive information, enabling exploitation, or unnecessarily putting users at risk.

Any disclosure must also respect applicable laws, contractual obligations, and third-party disclosure policies.

8. Handling Sensitive Information

During an authorized assessment, testers may encounter confidential or personal information.

Such information must be handled securely, accessed only when necessary and authorized, and must not be shared with unauthorized individuals.

Evidence should be minimized and protected throughout the assessment and reporting process.

9. Testing in Controlled Environments

For educational demonstrations, practice, and research, we encourage the use of intentionally vulnerable applications, local virtual machines, authorized labs, and dedicated training environments.

Examples include educational security labs and systems specifically designed for cybersecurity practice.

Users must follow the rules and authorization requirements of each training platform.

10. No Automatic Authorization

Reading this policy, visiting our website, or contacting DC DEVELOPERS does not grant permission to test any website, application, network, or digital system.

Authorization must come from the relevant system owner or a person legally empowered to grant it.

11. Policy Updates

DC DEVELOPERS may update this policy to reflect changes in its security practices, services, or applicable requirements.

The latest version will be published on this page.

12. Contact and Security Inquiries

For questions about this policy or responsible security-related inquiries, contact DC DEVELOPERS.
